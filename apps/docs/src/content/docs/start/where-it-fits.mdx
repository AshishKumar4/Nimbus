Nimbus sits at a different point in the design space than microVM sandboxes
like E2B and Daytona, container platforms like Cloudflare's container
Sandbox SDK, and in-process stub filesystems.

<WhereItFits caption="No native Linux software; in return, fast boots, $0 idle cost, and a disk that persists across processes." />

MicroVMs and containers run native Linux software. Nimbus does not. In-process
stubs stay inside the host process and do not provide real toolchains. Nimbus
instead boots in ~0.7 s median with no warm pool, costs $0 while idle, and has
a 10 GB disk. In a sequential comparison it measured 2.6× faster than the
container Sandbox SDK: 0.74 s vs 1.95 s median. Vite HMR took 302 ms median.
The [benchmarks page](/docs/fine-print/benchmarks/) has the method and caveats.

Nimbus's userland began as [LIFO OS](https://github.com/lifo-sh/lifo) by Sanket Sahu: the
shell interpreter, the coreutils, and the Node.js shim. Nimbus carries that
userland onto Cloudflare Durable Objects and adds per-process isolates, the
SQLite-backed 10 GB disk, port routing, and the in-session toolchains.

Workloads that need Docker, native wheels, GPUs, or raw TCP listeners are
out of scope. The full "no" list is in
[capabilities & limits](/docs/fine-print/capabilities/).
