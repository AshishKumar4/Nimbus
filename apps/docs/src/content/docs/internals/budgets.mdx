title: Budgets & limits
description: The platform numbers the fabric is designed around — and the engineering moves that turn hard caps into segments.
---

Nimbus doesn't fight its platform's limits; it's *shaped* by them. This page
lists the constraints (Cloudflare's published figures) and the specific
design move each one produced.

## The numbers

| Constraint | Published figure | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| Isolate memory | 128 MiB per isolate | supervisor DO and every facet alike |
| Durable Object SQLite storage | 10 GB per object | the session filesystem |
| CPU time | 30 s per invocation by default, raisable to 5 min via `limits.cpu_ms` ([Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/), [DO limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/limits/)) | every request/RPC into an isolate |

One nuance worth knowing: a raised `cpu_ms` applies to the supervisor DO's
invocations. Facet isolates get the ~30 s per-invocation budget regardless
of the deployment's configured limit, which is why long facet work is
sliced into phases below.

Nimbus intentionally publishes **only** Cloudflare's official figures here —
no folklore numbers, no unofficial measurements.

## What each limit produced

### 128 MiB → the multi-isolate process model

A hard per-isolate cap would normally be the ceiling on what an app can be.
Nimbus's answer is the OS answer: **128 MiB is a segment, not a ceiling.**

- Every process gets its own isolate, so its budget is its own.
- Heavy apps span isolates, joined by loopback (opencode: serve + TUI, two
  processes, two budgets).
- The supervisor never materializes heavy artifacts; module-map staging is
  offloaded out of the DO's isolate.
- Facet OOM is a clean, catchable error. The supervisor observes it and the
  session survives.

### 10 GB → one disk, owned by the DO

The session filesystem is sized by the platform's SQLite storage: **10 GB
per session**, chunked, atomically published, and streamed into via credit
backpressure so no writer — however parallel — can exceed the supervisor's
memory while filling it ([inside the disk](/docs/internals/disk/)).

### CPU budgets → phases and continuations

Long jobs are sliced to fit invocation budgets instead of hoping:

- The git engine runs fetch, index, and checkout as separate phases, each
  with a fresh budget, and checkout advances by bounded continuation
  ([the git engine](/docs/internals/git-engine/)).
- npm installs fan out across peer DOs, turning one big budget problem
  into many small ones.
- The known remaining ceiling (index-pack on 75,000+-object clones) is
  documented, not hidden.

## What idle costs

Nothing. Sessions hibernate: the Durable Object is evicted, the WebSocket is
hibernatable, the SQLite disk stays. Reconnect and the supervisor wakes with
files, process records, and shell state. The "$0 when idle" claim is a
direct consequence of DO hibernation, not a billing trick.

## Session-level bounds

Beyond platform numbers, Nimbus bounds its own surfaces: process logs,
input queues, output rings, LRU content caches, staged content generations,
and write-credit pools are all capped. The OS spec's standing rule is that
nothing user-driven may grow unbounded inside the supervisor. One
product-level bound worth knowing: a session allows **one active terminal
owner at a time** (sequential reconnect/share preserves state).
